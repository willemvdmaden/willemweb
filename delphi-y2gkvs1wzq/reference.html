Taxonomy of LLM self-explanations: reference sheet

Properties of a self-explanation, as shown to a user, that a designer can set through prompt, pipeline, or interface choices.

7 dimensions, 25 subdimensions. Every explanation takes exactly one characteristic per subdimension.

How to read this sheet

  • Dimension: a group of related properties. There are 7. Example: Surface.
  • Subdimension: one property. There are 25. Example: length.
  • Characteristic: one of the values a subdimension can take. Example: short, medium, long. Every explanation takes exactly one characteristic per subdimension.

Reading the example pairs. Each dimension page shows two cards that answer the same question with the same content. Chips in the card header give that card's characteristic on each subdimension; shaded spans in the text show where each subdimension appears.

One test for two of the dimensions. Structural and Delivery are separated by a simple test. If you paste the explanation into a plain text file and the property is still visible, it is structural (ordering, whether alternatives are weighed). If it disappears, it is delivery (whether the explanation was shown on request, whether it could be edited).

1 Surface

Surface covers how the explanation looks on the page before its content is read: how much text there is, what kind of words it uses, and how it is laid out. Surface properties can be changed without changing a single reason.

Subdimensions of Surface
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1LengthThe amount of explanation textshort (one or two sentences)/medium (three to six)/long (more than six)
2VocabularyThe register of word choiceeveryday/mixed/technical
3FormattingThe typographic structure of the textrunning prose/lightly structured (a list or emphasis)/heavily structured (headers, tables, nested lists)

2 Epistemic stance

Epistemic stance covers how the explanation positions the assistant toward its own claims: how sure it says it is, whether that certainty attaches to the whole answer or to individual claims, and whether individual reasons are softened. It does not cover how the assistant addresses the user; that is Interpersonal stance.

Note. Expressed confidence is about the verdict; hedging is about the reasons. A response can be categorical in its verdict and heavily hedged in its reasons, or the reverse.

Subdimensions of Epistemic stance
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1Expressed confidenceHow certain the conclusion is presented to becategorical/qualified/explicitly uncertain/numeric
2Confidence granularityThe level at which confidence is attachedresponse level/claim level
3HedgingWhether individual reasons carry mitigating words (appears to, possibly, seems)none/light (one or two mitigators)/heavy (most reasons)

3 Interpersonal stance

Interpersonal stance covers how the explanation positions the assistant toward the user: who is presented as doing the reasoning, how formal and how warm the address is, whether the assistant sides with what the user brought to the conversation, and whether it uses rhetorical devices beyond stating reasons.

Subdimensions of Interpersonal stance
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1FramingWho is presented as doing the reasoningfirst person/impersonal/tool voice/user-directed
2RegisterFormality toward the userformal/neutral/casual
3WarmthAffective stance toward the userwarm/neutral
4Agreement with user's positionStance toward the premise or view the user broughtchallenges/neutral/accommodates/actively affirms
5Persuasive appealsWhether the text uses rhetorical devices beyond stating reasons (authority, social proof, urgency, emotion)none/present

4 Structural

Structural covers the form of the reasoning as it appears on the page: whether it follows one path or weighs alternatives, whether every claim gets a reason, whether it says why this rather than something else, where the conclusion sits, and how the shown text relates to the model’s reasoning. Test: a property is structural if it survives pasting the explanation into a plain text file.

Subdimensions of Structural
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1Reasoning formThe path of the reasoninglinear/branching (alternatives considered)
2CompletenessWhether every claim in the answer has a stated reasonfull/partial
3Contrastive formWhether the explanation says why this rather than an alternativenon-contrastive/contrastive
4OrderingPosition of the conclusion relative to the reasonsconclusion first/reasons first/interleaved
5Trace formRelation of the shown text to the model's reasoning processfull trace/summarized trace/post-hoc rationale

5 Delivery

Delivery covers when and how the explanation reaches the user: whether it comes before, with, or after the answer; whether it is shown by default or on request; whether more can be revealed in steps; through which channel; and what the user can do with it. Test: a property is delivery if it is lost when the explanation is pasted into a plain text file.

Note. In this pair the explanation text is identical on both cards. The cards differ only in how the interface delivers it.

Subdimensions of Delivery
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1TimingWhen the explanation appears relative to the answerbefore/with/after/adaptive to user state
2AvailabilityWhether the explanation is shown by defaultalways shown/on request
3Progressive disclosureWhether more detail can be revealed in stepssingle level/expandable
4ModalityThe channel of the explanationtext/voice/text with visual indicators
5InteractivityWhat the user can do with the shown reasoningread-only/navigable/editable

6 Source

Source covers what the explanation rests on and how the user can check it: whether the shown reasons come from the process that produced the answer or were generated afterward, and how external evidence is linked.

Note. Provenance is the design choice about where the explanation comes from. Whether the result is faithful to the model's actual computation is what that choice is meant to secure, not a characteristic a designer sets directly.

Subdimensions of Source
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1ProvenanceHow the explanation relates to the process that produced the answergenerated from the reasoning trace/generated post hoc/verified against the process
2Citations and groundingHow external evidence is linkednone/attribution (source named)/citation (source linked)/quotation (verbatim passage)

7 Semantic

Semantic covers what the explanation claims: whether the reasons are true and support the answer, and whether the explanation and the answer agree.

Subdimensions of Semantic
#SubdimensionDefinitionCharacteristics
1Correctness of reasoningWhether the stated reasons are true and support the answercorrect/partially correct/incorrect
2Consistency with answerWhether the explanation and the answer agreeconsistent/contradicts
